## New findings

1. **Blocking — The latency model and acceptance targets use the wrong presentation time.** Under §5.4, request k+1 is locked when k *starts* presenting. Even if k+1’s first chunk is ready three seconds later, the viewer cannot see it until k’s five-second slot ends. Thus, when production keeps pace, L=0 has `TVC ≥ A + U(0,5) + 5 + I`; L=1 adds another five seconds. The §6.4 illustrative medians with `A=I=0.5` are at least **8.5 s and 13.5 s**, rather than 6.5 s and 11.5 s. The student for k+1 also needs k’s final KV commit; the supplied `runner.py:299–323` loop processes requests sequentially. A ready-time model must include that dependency and then take the later of *ready* and *scheduled presentation*. Revise §6.4 and recalculate §8’s TVC and TFMR targets from traced playback times.

2. **Major — The forward-correction deadline is still optimistic.** With L=0, `Pₖ₊₁ = Vₖ`; a VLM verdict based on any frame actually presented from k arrives too late for k+1. To reach k+2, the **VLM, correction synthesis, script compilation, moderation, and text encoding** must all finish before its lock. A failure seen in k’s last chunk leaves roughly 0.7 seconds of the nominal five-second slot; a slower check enters k+3 or later. §5.4’s “otherwise k+2” is therefore false. Measure and report the full verdict-to-lock chain, allow arbitrary later requests in the rule, and test the RQ2 endpoint using those timestamps.

3. **Major — The H3 prompt and voice control contract needs a concrete demonstration.** §5.3 says BlockScript matches TaoMate’s prompt convention, while the supplied H3 code accepts a **string per five-second request**; the notes describe H3-specific multimodal text and `<d>…</d>` dialogue tags. The six-field convention cited is from the LTX TaoMate paper. H3 has no voice reference, and its base-H3 audio-only teacher produces the published speech. Show one compiled H3 prompt and its teacher input, then make E1 test exact spoken content and speaker identity across prompt changes, seeds, and session resets. Pre-generation script moderation alone does not verify what the model actually says; specify how generated speech is checked before a live listener hears it, or state and evaluate the added delay.

4. **Major — RQ5 lacks the comparator and ascertainment needed for its claims.** RQ5 predicts lower false-user-fact rates with non-inferior recall, but §7.3 names no matched untyped-memory arm. Sharing a “memory service” across A1 and A2 does not establish that comparison. Add a baseline with the same retrieval budget and workload. The exact one-sided bound from 300 independent users is mathematically appropriate **if zero spoken leaks are ascertained**; ASR plus a spot listening audit can miss leaks. Audit all relevant generated speech, or validate detection sensitivity and adjust the claim to what was observed.

5. **Major — RQ4 cannot attribute long-session gains to the ledger.** §7.4 compares “ledger + text correction” and “ledger + reset” with verbatim canon repetition. Each comparison changes multiple mechanisms, and reset also changes the generator state and creates a seam. Add at least a ledger-only arm and matched correction/reset controls, with prompt budget and reset frequency held comparable. The A2-minus-ledger arm in E2 does not answer the 30–60-minute RQ4 question.

6. **Major — The cross-session bandit is part of S1 but is neither isolated nor protected from evaluation order.** §5.2 updates UCB1 between sessions using model scores and user feedback; §7.2 has no bandit ablation or rule freezing its policy during paired A1–A2 trials. Performance could change with condition order, and style exploration could change the canonical persona. Define canon-preserving arms, the unit and amount of bandit training data, and a frozen-policy evaluation or a separately randomized bandit ablation. Co-Director’s original reward updates are factor-specific across three axes, rather than 36 independent arm estimates. [Co-Director paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24842)

7. **Minor — One source characterization remains inaccurate.** §2.1 says all four Google-suite systems orchestrate frozen API models. VQQA also evaluates locally run CogVideoX-5B through Diffusers; its result is broader than API-only orchestration. The CANVAS storyboard qualification, A²RD overhead scope, and VQQA original-prompt selection are otherwise represented appropriately. [VQQA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12310), [CANVAS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13452), [A²RD paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06924)
