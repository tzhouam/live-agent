**Overall verdict: not ready.** E0–E6 cover the intended areas, but the central comparison, H-core test, and several success criteria need tighter definitions before the proposal is reviewable as an experimental plan. This review is limited to the inlined evidence.

1. **Blocking — H-core’s proposed comparator does not test “no external layer.”** Section 7.4 calls A1−mem that comparator, yet A1 still has an external LLM controller, and its dialogue history is carried across session restarts. Both conflict with §1.2’s definition. Specify a comparator that meets the definition, or narrow H-core to the memory service actually removed. **CP3: new issue.**

2. **Blocking — the primary compliance result lacks an independent ground truth.** A2 acts on monitor verdicts, while E2 also uses judged compliance as its outcome. E1 validates event judges on a 20% human sample, but E2 does not specify blinded human adjudication, treatment of `unknown`, or a locked split of scenes, prompts, and thresholds. A 10-point gain could reflect condition-dependent judge error. Lock evaluation data after calibration and validate the E2 outcome judge independently of the online monitor.

3. **Major — required response metrics are incomplete.** First audible speech can be a filler, so it is not *time to first meaningful response*. Camera TVC does not define *time to first correctly conditioned visual response* across shots and task types. Define both from the shared presentation timeline, along with end-to-end p50/p95 boundaries. Instruction following, multi-step success, correction, memory, stream stability, and compute otherwise have explicit measures.

4. **Major — power reasoning treats correlated trials as independent.** E2’s 300 pairs are 60 tasks repeated over five seeds; the stated McNemar calculation does not account for task clustering. It also does not cover FCR, ablations, or rare leaks. E3’s 20 users cannot establish a cross-scope leak rate below 1% without a specified, adequately sized set of independent opportunities. Give detectable effects and sample plans for each primary claim; retain users and tasks as clustering units.

5. **Major — denominators and censoring remain ambiguous.** FCR has no stated denominator or completion-claim coverage; a system could improve it by making no completion claims. Refusals, superseded instructions, `unknown` verdicts, and never-compliant attempts need explicit outcome rules. A censoring rate ≤5% alone does not make a TVC p95 identifiable; the restricted mean also needs a fixed horizon. **CP3 finding 15 is only partially resolved.**

6. **Major — RQ4 and RQ6 cannot support their stated conclusions yet.** A0 has no world shot, so its failure on scene-grounded tasks largely follows from the task definition. H4b calls for MOS non-inferiority without a margin or corresponding sample-size calculation. H6 proposes cross-shot similarity using a face embedding for one shot and a person re-identification embedding for the other, without a common calibrated score. Add a meaningful world-shot comparison and human-validated identity measures; test behavioral and scene-state consistency over the long sessions too.

7. **Major — the gated latency calculation needs a precise presentation event.** C4 says generation of chunk \(n+L+1\) starts when chunk \(n\) is “presented,” but does not say whether that means presentation starts or finishes. For \(L=1\), those choices change the claimed \(L P\) wait and the illustrative TVC budget. M0’s \(T\le P\) rule also ignores jitter and transport stalls. Define the acknowledgement edge and test queue boundedness under measured cadence distributions. **CP3 finding 12 is only partially resolved.**

8. **Major — erasure promises to cancel work the stated API cannot cancel.** Section 5.6 says in-flight IR items and queued prompts referencing erased content “are cancelled”; §5.3 says sent events cannot be withdrawn and an in-flight chunk cannot be redirected. Distinguish unsent cancellation, sent-event supersession, and media already generated or presented, then specify what a restart can actually clear. **CP3 finding 11 is not adequately resolved.**

9. **Minor — the record and numerical labels need cleanup.** Section 14 says the proposal went through all mandatory checkpoints while this CP4 review is still underway; the log anticipates CP4 re-review of CP3. D-001 calls the second scenario a *public* livestream, whereas the proposal and PROJECT.md specify a private test with no public broadcast. Trial counts and run counts in §7 are proposed design quantities but lack the proposal’s promised `[target]` or `[estimate]` labels. No local result is presented as measured.

The other CP3 resolutions are substantially reflected in the proposal, notably the shared A1/A2 control substrate, camera target and velocity semantics, prompt-path qualification, and the distinction between event acknowledgement and displayed compliance.
