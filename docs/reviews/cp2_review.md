1. **Blocking — G1 and contribution 2 overclaim novelty.** [WorldMark](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21686) already defines and measures *response latency* after a control switch, alongside stability, including third-person cases. TVC can still contribute if defined as **wall-clock instruction arrival to correctly displayed content**, including generation, transport, buffering, and semantic compliance. The text should distinguish that measure from WorldMark’s latency within the generated video.

2. **Blocking — the proposed demo undershoots the stated digital-persona goal.** A first-person explorer with voice-over has no visible persona; a distant third-person explorer does not test face, gesture, or lip sync. The quoted audit supports camera motion, not controlled character behavior. Keep a visible-persona requirement and test either a composited audio-driven avatar in a LingBot scene or a second avatar-focused track alongside the world-model experiment. Report where the two streams fail to stay coherent.

3. **Major — the public-path capability claim is sound only with a precise scope.** The quoted registry, coordinator, and WS error-handler paths support “only SE(3) camera **interactions** mid-session” as a code-read conclusion, not an executed result. Request-scoped camera scripts also exist; the tick path permits prompt switching. An event ID acknowledges application of a control, **not visible compliance**. The contradictory prompt-update benchmark makes one actual WS prompt-update test important before treating the predicted `interaction_failed` result as observed.

4. **Major — several capability and latency labels need tightening.** The ≈549 ms/chunk result is upstream-reported for a particular latest stack that includes optimizations described as selective or experimental; the matrix presents it as a general USP4 figure. The ≈0.6–1.15 s camera response is a derived *earliest chunk-delivery estimate*, not TVC: it omits queued playback and visual inertia. Likewise Wan-Streamer’s ≈550 ms total includes an **assumed** 350 ms network component. Audio/TTS integration and an external avatar path are engineering options; class D properly applies to *native LingBot lip, face, and audio conditioning*, not every way to show a speaking persona. WS rewind or snapshot also deserves “research/feasibility unknown” rather than an unqualified C.

5. **Major — §1.1 is fairer than the original blanket hypothesis, but its falsification test is loose.** X-Streamer, Wan-Streamer, LPM, and Genie 3 demonstrate relevant reasoning or events, while the cited evidence does not establish cross-session correction and provenance. “None provides all three” is a survey finding, not proof of absence. Define test horizons, correction and deletion cases, visible-compliance thresholds, and what counts as an “external state layer.” “Internal state” also mixes generator KV with the thinker’s dialogue context.

6. **Major — contribution 1 is the strongest candidate, but its prior art is understated.** Capability-aware planning resembles [SayCan’s measured affordances](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01691); feedback-driven replanning resembles [Inner Monologue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05608). The defensible claim is the **combination of calibrated stochastic-generator affordances, deadline-bound visual verification, and honest failure reporting under streaming latency**. Compare against a prompt-only planner, a capability-aware open-loop planner, and a verifier without calibrated affordances. G2’s “verify-and-refine exists only offline” is too broad without a targeted search for online visual-feedback agents.

7. **Major — G4–G10 vary substantially in novelty.** G4/contribution 3 is a video adaptation of [PACE’s playback-aligned context](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07631); its distinctive part needs client-reported presentation times and tests of video referents under interruption. G6–G7/contribution 4 combine existing provenance, access-control, and deletion ideas from Generative Agents, Zep/Graphiti, GateMem, and execution-state unlearning; a broadcast, cross-modal leakage evaluation could be novel. G8 is an ambitious model-training direction, not a near-term claim. G9 needs comparison with visual and multimodal memory work already listed in the supporting note. G10/contribution 5 should say **no single benchmark found covering the whole combination**: [WBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25874) already covers multi-turn subject actions and event editing, and WorldMark covers response dynamics.

8. **Major — interruption and evaluation need sharper operational definitions.** G5 says there is “no mid-chunk abort,” while the audit documents `session.stop` and request abort. The unresolved capability is *redirecting an in-flight chunk while retaining the session*, distinct from cancelling it. For TVC, record instruction receipt, control acceptance, generation start, mux completion, client presentation, and independently judged visible onset. Include failures and “never complied” cases; otherwise the latency result will be biased toward successes. Compare visual judges with blinded human labels and report their error and runtime cost.

9. **Major — material neighbors in the supporting notes disappear from the main synthesis.** At minimum position the proposal against WBench and WorldMark for evaluation; VideoFDB and MIDAS for nonverbal listening and duplex visual behavior; StreamingVLM for monitor cost; and MemoryArena and LiViBench for action-driving and livestream memory. Their omission makes the novelty and baseline choices look narrower than the team’s own search.

10. **Minor — the matrix and reference-agent treatment need different repairs.** The reference agent is appropriately marked qualitative and the H3 provenance unverified. The observed ≈15–20 s tea response should remain explicitly a single, unaligned observation. The matrix has most requested concepts, but **not every row has a direct URL, an explicit control interface, and latency plus hardware**; §2.4 in particular lacks those fields. Mark “not stated” per cell rather than relying on a section-wide arXiv URL convention. The LingBot KV description should distinguish the **recent ~2.25 s window** from the additional, temporally separate sink; calling their combined retained state “about 2.25 s” is misleading.

**Recommendation:** retain contribution 1 as the central research claim, with TVC as its measurement protocol. Gate the persona demo on showing a recognizable speaking character; otherwise the experiment would validate a camera-controlled scene agent rather than the owner’s intended digital persona.
